TOMATO
======

INSTALLING
Open Tomato right from this window. It moves itself to the Applications
folder and restarts from there. Without administrator rights, Tomato goes
to the Applications folder inside your home folder. Once it runs, its icon
appears in the menu bar at the top right of the screen.

You can also install Tomato by hand. Drag its icon onto the Applications
folder next to it and launch Tomato from the Applications folder.

IF macOS WON'T OPEN THE APP
The app isn't notarized by Apple, so on the first launch macOS may show
a warning ("cannot verify the developer" or "Apple could not verify it is free
of malware"). You only need to allow it once.

  • On macOS 15 and later, close the warning with "Done", open System
    Settings, go to Privacy & Security, scroll down, click "Open Anyway"
    next to Tomato and confirm.

  • On macOS 14, right-click Tomato in Finder, choose "Open", then click
    "Open" again in the warning.

  • If that didn't help, drag Tomato onto the Applications folder, open
    Terminal and run:
        xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Tomato.app

THE MENU BAR ICON
A click on the icon opens Tomato's panel. A right click or a Control-click
opens the menu: start, pause and stop, settings, checking for updates,
About and Quit.

THE TIMER
Tomato counts pomodoro time. The icon is a tomato cut in two like a
kitchen timer: marks round the belt of the upper half, one pointer on the
lower half. At rest the tomato stands still; while a timer runs its upper
half keeps turning smoothly and the time left stands beside it in a capsule
(Settings can hide it). In the panel you set the length
with a ruler from 1 to 60 minutes (drag it, use the wheel, or swipe with
two fingers on the trackpad) or with the values 5, 10 and 25. The "..."
button opens your own values, which you write in Settings. Then Start,
Pause, Resume and Stop. When time is up, Tomato rings three times like an
alarm, at the Mac's normal volume, shows a notification and flashes the icon. macOS asks for the permission to
show notifications at the first start; without it, the sound and the
flashing stay. The timer survives a restart of the app and of the Mac.

LANGUAGE
Tomato works in Russian and English. In Settings (⌘,) choose "Same as
System", "Русский" or "English". With "Same as System" Tomato takes the
first suitable language from the list in System Settings > General >
Language & Region. A new language takes effect after a restart, and the
"Restart" button appears right under the choice.

UPDATES
Tomato looks for a new version on GitHub at launch, every three hours and
after the Mac wakes up. It downloads a new version by itself, checks its
signature and installs it when Tomato is idle. With "Install updates
automatically" turned off in Settings, the icon gets a dot instead, and
Tomato's panel shows a card with "Later" and "Update".

OPEN AT LOGIN
Tomato opens at login to install updates on time. To stop it, turn off
"Open at login" in Settings.

REQUIREMENTS
macOS 14 or later, a Mac with Apple Silicon or Intel.
